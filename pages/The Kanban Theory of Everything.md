- # The Kanban Theory of Everything
- tags:: [[kanban boards]] [[productivity]] [[task management]] [[prioritization]]
- date:: [[August 18th, 2025]]
- There are a lot of different frameworks for prioritisation. Most do not work for me, here is one that does.
It's a version of the important, urgent matrix, which you may have seen.
[diagram of two axes labelled "important" from bottom to top and "urgent" from left to right. So the upper right hand quadrant is important urgent, etc. The general strategy is to do the important urgent things then the important non urgent things, with a few urgent things thrown in if they are meaningful to you]
## How it Works
I array tasks on kanban boards labelled "thought about once" "thought about twice" "thought about three times" etc.
The rules are pretty simple, if I think about a task once it goes on "thought about once". If I think about it again, it goes to thought about twice and so on.
If I add a new task it can go in some area other than "thought about once" eg "thought about three times" if it seems particularly important. 
- ## Weekly Planning
- When I am choosing which tasks to do for my week, I think about what obvious *need* to do - the things which I'd do regardless - a tax return, a grant application I've just heard about, but I also sprinkle through with a few things which I've thought about 5 or 6 times
- This means that over time I eventually do the things that I keep thinking of doing, even if they never seem that important on a given week. Usually I endorse this.
- ## Beyond Tasks
- I also apply this to my reading and films, slowly shuffling books, films and blogs up the ordering.
- ## Success Stories
	- Here are the kind of tasks this helped me to do sooner:
		- Record an interview with my granny before she died
		- Enter the metaculus AI forecasting competition earlier than I otherwise would have
- ## Why This Works
	- This system captures the natural importance of recurring thoughts
	- It prevents important-but-not-urgent tasks from being perpetually delayed
	- It creates a natural prioritization based on mental persistence rather than artificial urgency
	- It respects that some tasks gain importance through repetition of thought
