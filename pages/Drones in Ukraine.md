Before the full-scale invasion, Ukraine had already developed several reconnaissance and attack UAVs, such as the Leleka-100, but production volumes were relatively modest.

The invasion in February 2022 triggered an urgent need for drones, leading to a surge in both government and grassroots manufacturing efforts.

By late 2023, Ukraine set a target to produce at least one million drones in 2024.

2024: Production Boom

Ukraine exceeded its 2024 target by producing over 2.2 million drones, more than doubling the initial goal.

Of these, over 1.5 million were first-person-view (FPV) drones, which became a mainstay of Ukrainian frontline operations.

Domestically produced drones accounted for more than 96% of all UAVs used by Ukrainian forces in 2024, reflecting a highly localized and responsive supply chain.

Monthly production soared from around 20,000 drones at the start of 2024 to about 200,000 per month by January 2025, marking a tenfold year-on-year increase.

2025: Ambitious Expansion

Ukraine aims to double its output to 4.5 million drones in 2025, with the majority being FPV drones.

The government has allocated more than $2.5 billion for drone procurement and development, with additional funding from international partners, charities, and crowdfunding.

Production now includes not only FPV drones but also long-range strike drones, reconnaissance UAVs, kamikaze aircraft, and advanced models with increased electronic warfare (EW) resistance.

Ukraine plans to produce at least 30,000 long-range drones in 2025, further diversifying its UAV portfolio.

Key Trends and Innovations

Over 500 companies are now engaged in drone production, with more than 240 certified projects and rapid feedback loops between frontline troops and manufacturers for continuous improvement.

The industry is highly adaptive, with new models and upgrades quickly tested and deployed in combat conditions.

Ukraine’s approach-minimizing bureaucracy, fostering innovation, and leveraging both state and grassroots efforts-has become a model for modern warfare.

Ukraine
Ukraine plans to deploy an average of 2,500 large long-range drones per month in 2025. This is based on a stated annual target of 30,000 long-range drones for the year.

Additionally, Ukraine aims to produce and deploy about 250 cruise and missile drones per month, totaling 3,000 annually.

These figures are part of Ukraine's ambitious defense strategy, supported by significant domestic and international investment.

Russia
Russia is deploying approximately 2,000 to 4,200 Shahed-type long-range drones per month in early 2025.

In January 2025, Russian forces used 2,507 Shahed-type drones; in February, 3,907; and in March, 4,198.

The monthly average for the first quarter of 2025 is between 2,500 and 4,200 Shahed-type drones, with March showing the highest intensity.

These drones are used in regular, large-scale attacks, with some days seeing over 200 launched in a single night.
