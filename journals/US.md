    - Sam Hammond
    - They want a recession to lower yields. As part of tariff negotiations, they then want creditor nations to swap US bonds held by their central banks for 100-year "century bonds." These may or may not be gold convertible or pegged to a basket of commodities. The idea is to then lock-in a more favorable version of the US reserve currency; a kind of partial de-dollarization designed to correct payment imbalances without losing the dollar's reserve status altogether. They believe this requires a short-run period of economic pain, felt primarily through a large asset repricing (deflated stock and real estate values), but will ultimately redound in the form of a domestic investment boom.
